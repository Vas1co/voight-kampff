What is the Voight-Kampff test?

In Blade Runner (1982), based on Philip K. Dick's novel Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?, the Voight-Kampff test is used to tell humans from replicants. It is not a lie detector. The examiner asks emotionally provocative questions and the machine measures involuntary responses: pupil dilation, blush, pulse and how long you take to answer. Empathy is what it looks for.

How this game works

  • Baseline first. The machine records your resting readings before any question.
  • Seven questions. Each one is a short scenario with four possible answers and a time limit. Answer with the keys 1–4 or by clicking.
  • Raw data, no verdict. The screens show only deviations from your baseline. The machine never decides.
  • Unreliable instruments. Calibration drifts and anomalies happen, as they would with real hardware.
  • The examiner decides. At the end you receive a stamped report: human, replicant, or undetermined.

Atmosphere

A fluorescent tube flickers, a ceiling fan turns slowly, the observation glass is dark, and sometimes someone walks past the door. The game is built for headphones; subtitles are always on, and there is a reduced-motion setting.

Free, no download

The test runs in any modern browser on desktop or mobile. No account, no installation. The interface is in Turkish; the test takes about five minutes.
